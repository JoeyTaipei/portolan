SECTION 1 · OBJECTIVE OF THE MEETING
  • Establish credibility as a "non-red" foreign supplier that fills a capability gap, not a competitor to members.
  • Confirm the membership path for a foreign company, and what local-content partners it requires.
  • Ask for introductions to integrators positioned for the 2026–27 military drone procurement.
SECTION 2 · THE STAKEHOLDER: REMIT, STRUCTURE, INFLUENCE
  • What it is. Taiwan's main drone-industry alliance, with more than 260 member companies as of January 2026.[1]
  • International ties. Cooperation agreements with the Japan Drone Consortium, a Lithuanian partner, Poland's PISB and the UK's ADS (July 2026). Foreign links run through partnerships more than direct membership.[2][3]
  • Why it matters. The convening point for the "non-red" supply chain. Members are likely bidders and subcontractors in the 48,750-drone procurement for 2026–27, which bans Chinese parts but allows foreign suppliers for technology Taiwan lacks.[4]
SECTION 3 · LIKELY VIEWS ON [CLIENT]

Likely welcoming if [CLIENT] is framed as strengthening members' bids. Taiwan's Audit Office found the current counter-drone system cannot detect or jam the 5.1 GHz band used by newer PLA drones[5], a gap [CLIENT]'s sensors address. Expect caution about anything that looks like replacing local makers, and questions about export-licence status.

SECTION 4 · ELEVATOR PITCH
"[CLIENT] builds RF detection for small drones, including the newer frequency bands. We want to help Taiwanese integrators win counter-drone programs, with a fully non-red supply chain and local assembly through alliance members. We're here to learn how to contribute, not to compete."

Delivery note: lead with Taiwan's capability gap and members' success, not [CLIENT]'s size.
