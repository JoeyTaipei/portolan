# Government and trade-association export / market-entry services for SMEs (fact sheet, as of 4 Nov 2026)

Scope: what each service delivers, what it costs, what outcomes it reports, and what users complain about. The lens is Portolan's niche: drone and defence-tech startups going US→Taiwan and Taiwan→US. Every price or number has an inline source. Dates are given where information is older than 2025. "Business opportunities" figures from Taiwanese agencies are self-reported pipeline estimates, not signed contracts (see Inferences).

## 1. US Commercial Service / ITA: Gold Key, International Partner Search, Single Company Promotion, Featured U.S. Exporter, Country Commercial Guides

### Takeaway
Small US firms lost their discounts on 22 July 2026, when ITA moved to one cost-recovery price for all US companies. A small firm now pays $3,250 for a Gold Key (was $950) and $2,500 for an International Partner Search (was $750). Delivery is still a generalist embassy staffer who produces up to five "interested" contacts or meetings in one market, with 6–12 weeks of lead time. ITA's outcome reporting relies on clients choosing to report their results, and GAO has criticised it for that.

### Cited Findings
**Fee change (big 2026 news)**
- ITA published "Revisions to User Fees for Export and Investment Promotion Services/Events" (91 FR 37077, doc 2026-12507) on 22 June 2026. The revised schedule took effect on **22 July 2026**. The stated reason is OMB Circular A-25 full cost recovery, after "an independent cost study … determined that the agency was not fully covering its costs." — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/22/2026-12507/revisions-to-user-fees-for-export-and-investment-promotion-servicesevents); [trade.gov User Fees Update](https://www.trade.gov/user-fees-update) ("now in effect … implemented on July 22, 2026")
- Old vs new fees. Old fees are small / medium / large; new fees are for all US firms. Source: Federal Register Table 1/Table 3 — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/22/2026-12507/revisions-to-user-fees-for-export-and-investment-promotion-servicesevents)
  - Gold Key Service standard package: $950 / $2,300 / $3,400 → **$3,250** + direct costs. Add-ons: +$800 for more than 5 appointments or more than 8 hours. Identify & arrange only: $2,400. Arrange & attend: $360 per appointment. Arrange only: $225 per appointment.
  - International Partner Search: $750 / $1,750 / $2,250 → **$2,500**. IPS + Virtual Introductions: $900 / $2,100 / $2,700 → **$3,300**, plus $90 per introduction beyond 5.
  - Featured U.S. Exporter: $30 / $70 / $100 per market listing → **$100 per market listing**, annual renewal $50 per market, +$50 per language for translation.
  - Single Company Promotion, trade missions, customized market research, "all other services": $30 / $70 / $90 per staff hour → **$90 per staff hour** + direct costs.
  - Initial Market Check: $350 / $900 / $1,300 → **$1,950 per market**. Contact List: $150 / $350 / $450 → **$950**. International Company Profile (due diligence on one firm): Full $2,000, Partial $1,050. Trade Show Representation: $1,350. Website Globalization: $600. Webinar: $35 per webinar hour.
  - None of these fees cover direct costs (translation, transportation, venue, catering). ITA staff travel beyond 80 km or 2 hours from an ITA office is billed extra.
- Pre-2026 schedule (in effect from 1 Oct 2017), for reference: Gold Key small $950 / medium $2,300 / large $3,400; IPS $750 / $1,750 / $2,250; Featured U.S. Exporter (5 markets) $150 / $350 / $500; Single Company Promotion $30 / $70 / $90 per staff hour. — [ITA legacy fee schedule](https://legacy.trade.gov/fees/)
- The Connecticut Business & Industry Association (CBIA) wrote that the hikes "are expected to have the greatest impact on smaller exporters": a 242% increase for small firms on Gold Key, +41% for medium firms, and −4.4% for large firms. — [CBIA, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.cbia.com/news/economy/federal-export-agency-plans-major-fee-hikes/)
- According to a commentary blog, the notice says "The previously listed discounts for small and medium enterprises are no longer available." — [Cresques blog, 21 Oct 2026](https://cresques.com/blog/us-commercial-service-fees-2026-what-2500-dollars-buys-and-the-alternatives). This is a secondary source; its fee table matches the Federal Register.
- ITA says the cost study was based on FY2023 budget data plus a workload survey of all field units. ITA will accept comments on a rolling basis but "will not respond to comments." — [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/22/2026-12507/revisions-to-user-fees-for-export-and-investment-promotion-servicesevents)

**What is delivered**
- **Gold Key:** "matchmaking appointments with up to five interested partners in a foreign market." The full service covers finding and contacting matching firms, sending them the client's information, profiling the firms that respond, attending the appointments, and delivering a report with profiles and contacts. Eligibility: an export-ready US company whose goods or services are of US origin or have at least 51% US content. — [trade.gov Gold Key](https://www.trade.gov/gold-key-service)
- Some embassy versions of the Gold Key add a customised market and industry briefing before the meetings and an out-brief on follow-up strategy afterwards. — [US Embassy Turkmenistan](https://tm.usembassy.gov/gold-key-service-matchmaking/); [US Embassy Lithuania](https://lt.usembassy.gov/commercial-services/)
- **IPS:** "a list of up to five partners/distributors that have expressed an interest." It covers outreach, sending the client's information, and a profile and contact report. The Virtual Introductions version adds conference calls with up to five of the contacts. — [trade.gov IPS](https://www.trade.gov/international-partner-search)
- **Single Company Promotion:** a promotional event such as a technical seminar, press conference, luncheon or reception. It includes logistics, a targeted direct-mail or email campaign, on-site support and a post-event debrief. It is priced at $90 per staff hour plus direct costs. — [trade.gov SCP](https://www.trade.gov/single-company-promotion)
- **Featured U.S. Exporter:** a listing on Commercial Service market websites, priced per market (fee above). I could not fetch the trade.gov FUSE page during this session (timeout).
- **Market intelligence** is published free on trade.gov. One example is the "Taiwan Drone Systems" brief (Aug 2025), which covers TEDIBOA, the MOEA goal of 15,000 drones per month and NT$30bn output by 2028, the NT$110m subsidy for AI image chip modules and low-cost flight control boards, and "opportunities for U.S. drone companies to collaborate with Taiwan-based industries." — [trade.gov Taiwan Drone Systems](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/taiwan-drone-systems)

**Turnaround**
- A Gold Key "can take 6-8 weeks to complete." It pre-screens and organises "a day or two of meetings with 3-5 potential partners." — [Census Bureau blog, Jan 2013](https://www.census.gov/newsroom/blogs/global-reach/2013/01/gold-key.html)
- The Turkmenistan post requires requests "six weeks prior to the arrival date." — [US Embassy Turkmenistan](https://tm.usembassy.gov/gold-key-service-matchmaking/)
- For franchisors, meetings are arranged "within about eight to 12 weeks." Not every country offers the Gold Key. — [Global Franchise, Dec 2019](https://www.global-franchise.com/insight/why-franchisors-should-be-utilizing-the-u-s-commercial-service)

**Reported results**
- A Commercial Service official said it "facilitated $54 billion in U.S. export sales" worldwide in FY2011. — [Star Tribune, Jul 2012](https://www.startribune.com/he-puts-minnesota-firms-on-the-right-path-to-exporting/162404546/)
- The share of clients whose export objectives were met rose from 67% (FY2013) to 78% (FY2016). — [Performance.gov archive](https://obamaadministration.archives.performance.gov/content/global-markets-increases-percentage-companies-whose-export-objectives-are-met-11-and-clients.html)
- In its FY2010 GPRA measures, CS missed 4 of 6 targets. For example, "SME new-to-export effectiveness" came in at 2.28% against a 12.74% target, while "overall effectiveness" was 29.1% against an 11% target. — [GAO-11-909](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-11-909.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 2026 repricing changes the competitive picture for Portolan. A small US drone startup now pays $2,500–$3,250 for up to five "interested" contacts in Taiwan, before travel and interpreters. That is a concrete price anchor for Portolan's one-page report and stakeholder map.
- ITA's output is up to five names with profiles. It has no visible sourcing, no scoring and no stakeholder-level map of ministries, primes and integrators. It also excludes firms with under 51% US content, which may exclude US drone startups that use Taiwanese components.

### Gaps
- I found no current (2024–26) published success rate or satisfaction score specifically for the Gold Key or IPS.
- I did not verify FUSE deliverables on trade.gov (page timed out). I also did not verify whether Country Commercial Guides remain free, though they are typically free web content.
- Commercial Service capacity in Taiwan (AIT Commercial Section staffing, defence-sector specialists) was not researched.

## 2. TAITRA, InvestTaiwan and drone programmes (TEDIBOA)

### Takeaway
TAITRA's buyer-side services are free. It mainly runs trade shows (TADTE for aerospace and defence) with one-on-one procurement meetings, and it reports large "business opportunity" numbers. TEDIBOA is the MOEA's drone "national team." It grew from 50 to more than 260 members, signs MOUs abroad, runs pavilions at XPONENTIAL and other shows, and in 2026 is focused on getting members Blue/Green UAS certification for the US. InvestTaiwan offers a free single window for foreign investors.

### Cited Findings
**TAITRA general**
- TAITRA's sourcing services for foreign buyers are free. Taiwan Trade Centres say "All of our sourcing services are free-of-charge." — [TTC London LinkedIn](https://linkedin.com/company/taiwan-trade-centre-london)
- TTC San Francisco and Miami list their free services: B2B connection assistance (in-person or online meetings with Taiwanese suppliers), registration and **travel subsidies** for trade shows in Taipei (more than 30 specialised shows a year), and investment assistance. — [TTC San Francisco](https://www.linkedin.com/company/taiwan-trade-center-san-francisco); [TTC Miami](https://linkedin.com/company/taiwan-trade-center-miami-inc-)
- The Tokyo office lists free supplier introductions, free meeting arrangements and free use of the Taipei HQ meeting rooms. — [TAITRA Tokyo](https://tokyo.taiwantrade.com/note.jsp?id=3946)
- Online matching runs as 30-minute one-on-one video meetings with selected suppliers. — [TAITRA Matching Services](https://en.innovation.taitra.org.tw/matching-services)
- Taiwantrade.com, run by TAITRA under the Bureau of Foreign Trade: more than 660,000 product listings from 70,000 supplier members, free for international buyers, available in 14 languages, across 63 offices. — [TAITRA Taiwantrade](https://www.taitra.org.tw/en/News_Content.aspx?n=225&s=11952)

**TADTE 2025 (TAITRA's aerospace and defence show, 18–20 Sep 2025)**
- 490 companies from 15 countries, 1,500 booths and over 38,000 visitors from 87 countries. — [TAITRA annual report](https://annualreport.taitra.org.tw/en/industrialpromotion). Unmanned Systems Technology reports "more than 36,000 visitors" instead. — [UST](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2025/09/tadte-2025-record-attendance-global-collaboration-unmanned-innovation/)
- B2B matchmaking was run with TW-DIDA and TEDIBOA: 91 buyers from 20 countries, 502 meetings and **USD 72.8m "business opportunities."** The government procurement session drew 17 buyers from 9 countries (including the US, France and Israel), held 139 meetings with 68 Taiwanese firms and reported **USD 45.475m** in business opportunities. — [TAITRA annual report](https://annualreport.taitra.org.tw/en/industrialpromotion)
- Before the show, TAITRA announced more than 140 buyers from 30 countries registered for one-on-one meetings. — [TADTE news](https://www.tadte.com.tw/en/news/C84F0B5A4E8FE035/info.html?lt=table); [AmCham TOPICS](https://topics.amcham.com.tw/2025/10/tadte-2025-showcases-ai-driven-defense-and-expanding-u-s-partnerships/). UST reports that procurement meetings "reportedly generated business opportunities valued at more than US$200 million." — [UST](https://www.unmannedsystemstechnology.com/2025/09/tadte-2025-record-attendance-global-collaboration-unmanned-innovation/). These figures conflict with TAITRA's own annual-report numbers.
- 23 MOUs were signed during TADTE, mostly Taiwan–US and mostly about product distribution. NCSIST signed 5 unmanned-vehicle deals (AeroVironment, Anduril, MARTAC, Airshare, Northrop Grumman). — [DSET drone newsletter, Oct 2025](https://dset.tw/en/research/dset-drone-newsletter-eng1/)

**TEDIBOA (台灣卓越無人機海外商機聯盟)**
- The MOEA launched TEDIBOA on 10 Sep 2024. It is chaired by the chairman of AIDC (漢翔). Its stated aims are information sharing, building a complete industrial ecosystem, international collaboration and a "non-red supply chain." — [TEDIBOA About](https://www.tediboa.com.tw/en/About)
- Trade.gov described it as having over 100 members, led by AIDC with seven major companies including Mitac Advance Technology, Thunder Tiger, Coretronic and Geosat. — [trade.gov, Aug 2025](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/taiwan-drone-systems)
- Membership grew from 50 founding members to more than 260 by 22 Jan 2026, when it held its first general assembly. The **2026 priority is helping members obtain US certification (Green/Blue UAS)** under the new FCC rules, with ITRI, through "guidance instead of subsidies." By then it had ties with 11 drone organisations in 7 countries. — [工商時報, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.ctee.com.tw/news/20260122702203-430503)
- Outcomes: membership is over 260 companies, with MOUs with counterparts in 8 countries (US, Japan, Czechia, Poland, Ukraine, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania). The MOEA says the alliance "has already secured overseas orders worth a total of NT$4.25 billion." — [AmCham TOPICS, Apr 2026](https://topics.amcham.com.tw/2026/04/taiwans-drone-industry-take-off-but-can-it-scale/)
- An MOEA deck reports 267 firms that took part in 11 international drone shows (Germany, Poland, US, Japan), MOUs with 9 countries, and international cooperation and procurement talks "exceeding NT$10bn." It also describes the MOEA brokering foreign primes' demand for non-red parts: more than 10 Taiwanese firms got Anduril prototype or production orders, 5 firms are working with Skydio and 6 with Parrot. Anduril and Shield AI have set up sales and procurement offices in Taiwan. — [MOEA XPONENTIAL briefing (PDF)](https://www.moea.gov.tw/MNS/populace/news/wHandNews_File.ashx?file_id=125484)
- At XPONENTIAL 2026 (Detroit, 11–14 May), the "largest ever" Taiwan pavilion reported NT$1.3bn (~US$41m) in potential business opportunities. The MOEA cited Thunder Tiger's NT$240m order for 1,520 drones, and MOUs were signed with a Michigan drone alliance and the North Dakota Trade Office. — [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6367348). Company counts conflict: 35 companies according to Taiwan News/CNA and the MOEA deck, but "25家業者" in the IDA press release. — [IDA](https://www.ida.gov.tw/ctlr?PRO=news.NewsView&id=43211)
- Taiwan's drone output was NT$12.9bn in 2025, up more than 2.5× on 2024. Whole-drone exports rose from NT$140m to NT$2.95bn, about 21×. — [IDA](https://www.ida.gov.tw/ctlr?PRO=news.NewsView&id=43211)
- TEDIBOA signed an MoU with UK trade body ADS at Farnborough on 23 Jul 2026. At that point it had partnerships with "15 drone organizations across 8 countries." — [ADS Group](https://www.adsgroup.org.uk/knowledge/uk-taiwan-drone-industry-collaboration/)
- Sign-up for its 2026 international exchange programme goes through 國防產業發展協會 (TW-DIDA). — [TEDIBOA announcement, Aug 2025](https://www.tediboa.com.tw/Announcement)
- Most Taiwanese drone exports go to Czechia and Poland, with the US "a distant third." Exports are mostly dual-use, incomplete drones and components. — [AmCham TOPICS, Apr 2026](https://topics.amcham.com.tw/2026/04/taiwans-drone-industry-take-off-but-can-it-scale/)

**InvestTaiwan (for foreign firms entering Taiwan)**
- The InvesTaiwan Service Center offers a single window with a "project-oriented, dedicated case manager … customized start-to-finish service." It covers consulting, help finding land, plants or offices, incentive applications and coordination with agencies. — [InvestTaiwan](https://investtaiwan.nat.gov.tw/showPageengserviceCenter_12?lang=eng&search=serviceCenter_12); [InvestTaiwan portal](https://investtaiwan.nat.gov.tw/)
- A dedicated project manager is assigned for investments over US$15.4m (NT$500m). Company registration review takes two days. — [US State Dept 2025 Investment Climate Statement](https://www.state.gov/reports/2025-investment-climate-statements/taiwan)
- More than 50% of assistance cases are about finding land or factory space. — [AmCham TOPICS, Nov 2018](https://topics.amcham.com.tw/2018/11/aiming-to-serve-foreign-investors/)

### Inferences
- TAITRA and TEDIBOA are built to push Taiwanese supply outward (shows, pavilions, MOUs, catalogues). Their outcome figures are "potential business opportunities" or pipeline estimates and differ between sources, sometimes sharply (USD 72.8m+45.5m vs >USD 200m for the same TADTE). Portolan can position on source-traceable, per-stakeholder detail instead.
- A US startup entering Taiwan gets mostly free but supplier-oriented help from TAITRA, and investment-oriented help (land, registration) from InvestTaiwan. Neither offers a map of MND, NCSIST, primes and integrators as buyers or partners. The MOEA's own brokering of primes (Anduril, Skydio, Parrot) suggests the useful matchmaking happens ministry-to-company and is not productised.
- For Taiwanese firms entering the US, TEDIBOA's 2026 focus on Blue/Green UAS certification shows that certification and compliance pathways are a gating need. Portolan's reports should cover them.

### Gaps
- TEDIBOA membership fees and admission criteria were not found.
- I found no independent audit of TAITRA's or TEDIBOA's reported business-opportunity numbers, and no conversion rate to signed contracts.
- I found no published TAITRA fees for Taiwanese exporters, such as pavilion participation costs. Only the free buyer-side services are documented.

## 3. Comparable services: Enterprise Singapore, Austrade, UK DBT, EU Enterprise Europe Network, JETRO

### Takeaway
Most peer agencies give advice free and fund market entry through co-funding grants rather than selling matchmaking. Enterprise Singapore covers up to 70% of costs up to S$100k per market. Austrade's EMDG grants run A$30k–80k a year, matched 1:1. The UK's main chargeable service is OMIS at about £55 an hour. EEN and JETRO are free. That makes the US's new $2,500–$3,250 flat fees an outlier.

### Cited Findings
**Enterprise Singapore**
- The Market Readiness Assistance (MRA) grant covers up to 70% of eligible costs for local SMEs from 1 Apr 2026 (it was 50%). The cap is S$100,000 per company per new market, split into market promotion (S$20k), business development (S$50k) and market set-up (S$30k). Eligibility: at least 30% local equity, group turnover of S$100m or less or 200 or fewer employees, and new to the market (target-market sales of S$100k or less in each of the prior 3 years). — [EnterpriseSG MRA](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/financial-support/market-readiness-assistance-grant/)
- "EDG, MRA and PSG ceased on 29 September 2026. From 30 September onwards, apply … under the EDGE Grant." EDGE extends support to non-SMEs at up to 50% and removes the "new market" requirement. — [EnterpriseSG MRA](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/financial-support/market-readiness-assistance-grant/); [EnterpriseSG Budget 2026](https://www.enterprisesg.gov.sg/campaigns/budget-2026); [MTI](https://www.mti.gov.sg/newsroom/written-reply-to-pq-on-initiatives-and-strategies-to-help-singaporeans-and-enterprises-tap-into-fast-growing-markets-in-africa-and-middle-east/)
- Global Innovation Alliance schemes (startup market-access programmes, in-market experts) are funded at up to 70% for SMEs and 50% for non-SMEs from 1 Apr 2026 to 31 Mar 2029. Under the Double Tax Deduction for Internationalisation, the no-prior-approval cap rises from S$150k to S$400k from YA2027. — [Singapore Budget 2026 Annex B-1](https://cms.singaporebudget.gov.sg/assets/cf32e732-927d-4d50-85ff-a1353fa96919)

**Austrade**
- Tailored trade services: export readiness and strategy, market potential assessment, introductions to in-market service providers, business matching, in-market experiences, visa support letters and troubleshooting market access barriers. There is a 4-point eligibility check (ABN, net benefit to Australia, ethical business, best interest). — [Austrade Trade services](https://www.austrade.gov.au/en/how-we-can-help-you/programs-and-services/trade-services.html)
- EMDG maximum grants per year for 2025–26 and 2026–27: Tier 1 A$20k–30k, Tier 2 A$20k–50k, Tier 3 A$20k–80k, representative bodies up to A$50k. Grants must be matched with the firm's own funds, and the firm must be able to spend at least A$20k. — [Austrade EMDG criteria](https://www.austrade.gov.au/en/how-we-can-help-you/grants/export-market-development-grants/check-eligibility/criteria)
- EMDG Round 4 is closed and no round is currently open. — [Austrade EMDG](https://www.austrade.gov.au/en/how-we-can-help-you/grants/export-market-development-grants)
- A consultant site gives further eligibility detail (turnover under A$20m, a maximum of 8 grant years, minimum turnover by tier) and notes that a common route is "an EMDG Consultant who is paid on success." — [Treadstone](https://www.treadstone.com.au/emdg-export-grant-consulting/). This is a secondary source.

**UK Department for Business and Trade**
- "The majority of DBT support to exporters … is delivered at no cost." Its chargeable Overseas Market Introduction Service (OMIS) provides market intelligence and connections "at a rate of £55 per hour exclusive of VAT." — [UK Parliament written answer, 6 Jul 2023](https://api.parliament.uk/query/resource?uri=https%3A%2F%2Fid.parliament.uk%2FhrDvJcIH)
- OMIS covers bespoke research, partner identification, visit support and event preparation. A short distributor report starts "from £500 upwards." — [Coventry & Warwickshire Chamber (undated, DIT-era page)](https://www.cw-chamber.co.uk/international-trade/archive-pages/department-for-international-trade/overseas-market-research/); [OMIS portal](https://omis.trade.gov.uk/)
- One-to-one International Markets hub support needs turnover of £500,000 or more, an export-ready product and some export experience. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/access-international-market-export-support)
- Export Client Quality Survey 2024/25: satisfaction was 71% (down from 74%) and 66% said their needs were met (down from 69%). For the International Markets service, needs met fell from 67% to 57%. — [DBT ECS 2024–25](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4663e78effd97622f53b79/export-client-quality-survey-2024-to-2025.pdf)

**Enterprise Europe Network (EEN)**
- Funded by the European Commission, with more than 600 partner organisations in over 60 countries. Support is free. — [EU-Japan Centre EEN](https://www.eu-japan.eu/een)
- The public partnering-opportunities database listed 5,777 open profiles when checked (Nov 2026). — [EEN partnering opportunities](https://een.ec.europa.eu/partnering-opportunities)
- Example of reported outcomes: in FY24 the EU-Japan Centre (EEN in Japan) facilitated 2,067 meetings, which led to "14 partnership agreements signed" and 27 advisory achievements. — [EU-Japan Centre FY24 report](https://www.eu-japan.eu/sites/default/files/imce/FY24-achievement-report.pdf)

**JETRO**
- JETRO's Invest Japan Business Support Centers in 6 cities provide support "free of charge … (partially for a fee)," including a free temporary office for up to 50 business days. — [JETRO IBSC](https://www.jetro.go.jp/en/invest/jetros_support/ibsc/); [JETRO support](https://www.jetro.go.jp/en/invest/jetros_support/)
- JETRO USA says "All businesses services provided by JETRO are absolutely free." Services include consultations, a business development advisor in both the US and Japan, the TTPP business-matching database and personalised market research. — [JETRO USA](https://www.jetro.go.jp/usa/japan-external-trade-organization-services.html)

### Inferences
- Peer agencies mostly subsidise the SME's own spending (grants, tax deductions) rather than selling a deliverable. A Singapore SME could put up to 70% of a market-entry consultancy, which could include a Portolan-type service, against the MRA/EDGE grant. A UK SME pays about £55 an hour to DBT. This suggests an "eligible for grant reimbursement" angle for Portolan, unverified.
- EEN's published FY24 Japan figure is 14 partnership agreements from 2,067 facilitated meetings, a rough conversion of under 1%. That gives a baseline for how rarely matchmaking turns into deals.

### Gaps
- I did not find Austrade's current fee schedule for paid tailored services, if any. Secondary sources only say most core services are free.
- I did not find Enterprise Singapore's overseas-centre matchmaking deliverables or EEN-wide success statistics.
- I did not find a current confirmation that OMIS is still £55 an hour in 2026. The figure dates from Jul 2023.
- JETRO's paid elements ("partially for a fee") are not itemised.

## 4. AmCham Taiwan and similar chambers: what membership gives for market entry

### Takeaway
AmCham Taiwan sells networking, advocacy (the White Paper, the Washington Doorknock) and information. Membership costs about NT$72k in the first year for a company (joining fee plus annual dues). It has a Defense committee, but it offers no matchmaking or partner-search deliverable.

### Cited Findings
- Fees from AmCham's 2026 brochure (the table parses imperfectly). Company: NT$23,100 joining fee and NT$48,900 annual dues (Company–International NT$49,400), with additional members at NT$13,800. Corporate Sustaining: NT$88,700–89,200 a year. Individual: NT$17,300 joining fee and about NT$34,400 a year. Non-Resident: US$115 joining fee and US$743 a year. Young Professional: NT$12,200 a year. — [AmCham Taiwan brochure, Apr 2026 (PDF)](https://amcham.com.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/AmCham-Taiwan-Brochure-20260416.pdf)
- Scale: more than 500 businesses, over 1,100 executive members, 23 committees, over 150 events a year, founded 1951. — [AmCham brochure](https://amcham.com.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/AmCham-Taiwan-Brochure-20260416.pdf)
- The committees include **Defense**, Semiconductor and Technology. Each committee writes a position paper for the annual Taiwan White Paper (2026 edition released 16 June). — [AmCham Committees](https://amcham.com.tw/committees/)
- Benefits: networking (monthly luncheons, committee meetings, special access meetings), company exposure (TOPICS magazine), and advocacy (White Paper, Business Climate Survey, annual Doorknock trip to Washington). Members need not be American or live in Taiwan. — [AmCham Membership](https://amcham.com.tw/membership/); [AmCham FAQ](https://amcham.com.tw/about/faq/); [AmCham About](https://amcham.com.tw/about/)
- Committees can be set up as "Restricted Membership Committees" that limit who may join. — [AmCham Committee Guidelines (PDF)](https://amcham.com.tw/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Committee-Guidelines.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a foreign drone startup, AmCham is a place to meet people and get policy context, at roughly US$2.3k in the first year. It does not identify or introduce specific buyers. Portolan could complement it by naming which AmCham Defense-committee members or events are relevant to the startup.

### Gaps
- I did not research whether the Defense committee is restricted, or which companies sit on it.

## 5. Evidence on effectiveness (GAO/OIG audits, satisfaction surveys, academic evaluations)

### Takeaway
Rigorous firm-level studies find modest positive effects that build up over time. The effects are largest for small, new-to-export firms and for direct services such as finding distributors. Meta-analyses, however, find severe publication bias, and the effect on firms that already export is about 1–9%. Self-reported outcome data from US agencies is weak, with low survey response rates and under-reported deal values.

### Cited Findings
- Across 103–104 countries, each $1 of export promotion is associated with about $300 of additional exports for the median EPA. Returns diminish strongly ("small is beautiful"). EPAs work best where trade barriers are high and goods are heterogeneous. — [Lederman, Olarreaga & Payton (World Bank WP 4044, 2006)](https://doi.org/10.1596/1813-9450-4044); [J. Dev. Econ. 2010](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304387809000935)
- In Denmark, support raised the probability of exporting by 3.9 percentage points in the year of support and 5.9 points two years later (Munch & Schaur 2018). In Flanders it raised the probability by 8.5 points (Broocks & Van Biesebroeck 2017). "Bundled services … and the more direct measures, such as helping firms find distributors – are more effective." — [VoxEU/CEPR survey, Oct 2019](http://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/effectiveness-export-promotion-measures-survey)
- A systematic review for the UK Department for International Trade (DIT) found that export propensity rose 10.7% for small firms versus 3.5% for large firms, that impact "builds up" over years, and that sustained support beats one-off support. UKTI support was associated with 1.45% extra turnover growth. — [DIT systematic review (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6194cf618fa8f5037ffaa0a2/the-impact-of-publicly-funded-export-promotion-services-a-systematic-review-prepared-for-dit.pdf)
- A meta-analysis of 479 estimates found "significant and severe positive evidence of publication bias." The literature overstates the effect on export market entry. — [Demena, Applied Economics Letters 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/13504851.2024.2306185)
- On firms that already export, a meta-analysis of 951 estimates found export intensity 1–9% higher after correcting for publication bias, so practical significance is "limited." Service-type support matters more than financial support. — [Demena, JABES 2024](https://www.emerald.com/jabes/article/31/4/250/1230526/Does-export-promotion-enhance-firm-level-intensive)
- **GAO-11-909 (2011):** CS customer satisfaction was 84% (2009) and 82% (2010), but survey response rates were only 10% and 19%. CS reported the results without the margin of error, "potentially misleading readers." About 34% of CS export successes in 2008–2010 had no dollar value, and clients often did not comply with reporting clauses in purchase agreements. — [GAO-11-909](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-11-909.pdf)
- **GAO-09-144 (2009):** CS lacked reliable data on the fees it charged and on its customers. Most state trade offices thought SME fees for some services were "too high when compared with those charged by private sector providers." Other factors also affect SME uptake: service types, individualised attention and quality. — [GAO-09-144](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-09-144.pdf)
- **USAID CDIE (1999):** exporters surveyed "gave little to no credit to government service providers," crediting buyers and private partners instead. Effective services were "highly targeted, results-focused and time bound." — [McKean, IDB/USAID 1999](https://doi.org/10.18235/0006806)
- **Scotland (2023):** firms rated satisfaction at 7/10 on average, and 87% found the support relevant. Negative ratings cited irrelevant advice, delays and too little support for their sector. — [gov.scot evaluation, Nov 2023](https://www.gov.scot/publications/team-scotlands-export-promotion-support-evaluation/pages/10/)

### Inferences
- The evidence favours what Portolan does: direct partner identification, bundled and sustained support, and help for small first-time entrants. It also warns that agencies' own outcome claims are poorly measured. Portolan's debrief and tracker data could build a more credible outcome record than self-reported "export successes."

### Gaps
- I found no recent (2023–26) GAO or Commerce OIG audit of the Gold Key or IPS specifically, and no evaluation of the 2026 fee change's effect on demand.
- I found no evaluation of TAITRA or TEDIBOA effectiveness.

## 6. Common complaints and gaps

### Takeaway
The most consistent complaints are that services are generic and not sector-specific, that there is little follow-up, that some contact lists are out of date, and that high eligibility thresholds or fees exclude small firms. The single biggest barrier SMEs report is still "access to the right contacts."

### Cited Findings
- In DBT's 2024/25 survey, the top reason for dissatisfaction was that the service "did not do anything or did not help" (43%), then "not enough information or advice" (27%) and "did not meet expectations" (23%). Requested improvements centred on more follow-up from DBT and more sector-specific services (32%). Detractors wanted more sector-specific services (25%) and more knowledgeable staff (14%). The most common export barrier was "access to the right contacts, customers and networks" (41%). — [DBT ECS 2024–25](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6a4663e78effd97622f53b79/export-client-quality-survey-2024-to-2025.pdf)
- In the 2022–23 survey, dissatisfied firms cited no follow-up, advice suited to a different type of business, "not knowing what to do after dealing with DBT," and referral to irrelevant services. — [DBT ECS 2022–23](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/677e82ff22a085c5ff5c04af/export-client-quality-survey-2022-to-2023.pdf)
- "More generic types of services were felt to be of little benefit." One firm paid for a database of retailers and found "many of the contact details to be out of date and unusable." Another found a lead-generation agency lacked industry knowledge. — [DIT research on businesses' use of export support](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6304e60ad3bf7f366522bea6/understanding-businesses-use-of-export-promotion-support-services.pdf)
- Medium-sized firms are willing to pay only if support is "bespoke and tailored … good value for money, and available in a timely fashion." — [DIT medium-sized business research](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63061c94e90e0729dcbd7e7e/understanding-medium-sized-businesses-exporting-behaviour-and-the-role-of-DIT.pdf)
- Cost and access in the US: the 2026 removal of SME discounts (Gold Key +242% for small firms) and the 51% US-content rule. — [CBIA](https://www.cbia.com/news/economy/federal-export-agency-plans-major-fee-hikes/); [trade.gov Gold Key](https://www.trade.gov/gold-key-service)
- Older GAO findings: fees seen as too high versus private providers, and service quality and individual attention affect uptake. — [GAO-09-144](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-09-144.pdf)
- Results are capped at "up to five" contacts and depend on how precisely the client defines its ideal partner ("the vetting quality you get out is only as good as the criteria you put in"). — [trade.gov IPS](https://www.trade.gov/international-partner-search); [GrantCompass (secondary; still quotes the pre-2026 $950 fee)](https://grantcompass.co/grants/commerce-commercial-service-gold-key)
- The UK's one-to-one hub support requires turnover of £500k or more and prior export experience, which excludes pre-revenue startups. — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/access-international-market-export-support)
- Positive counterpoint: firms that used UK International Trade Advisers valued free, impartial, government-backed advice that "no other outlet" offers. — [DIT MSB research](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/63061c94e90e0729dcbd7e7e/understanding-medium-sized-businesses-exporting-behaviour-and-the-role-of-DIT.pdf)

### Inferences
- Portolan's sourced, sector-specific stakeholder map with concrete next steps per stakeholder, plus its tracker and debrief, addresses the top three complaints directly: generic output, out-of-date contacts and no follow-up. Government services still have something Portolan lacks: official credibility and embassy convening power. "Alongside" positioning (prepare the client before a Gold Key or TADTE procurement meeting and track the follow-up) looks plausible.

### Gaps
- I found few first-hand public complaints (forums, reviews) specifically about the Gold Key, TAITRA matchmaking or TEDIBOA. Most complaint evidence is UK survey data.
- I found no defence or drone-specific user feedback on any of these services. Export controls (ITAR/EAR for US→Taiwan) and how agencies handle them were not researched.
