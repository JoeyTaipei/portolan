# Commercial software and AI tools overlapping with Portolan (market entry, stakeholder mapping, procurement intelligence)

Research date: 2026-11-04. Prices are as published or reported on the date shown in each source. Many comparison pages below are written by competing vendors (GovTribe, Tractivity, Calven, BidSparq, ImportGenius, etc.); they are marked "(vendor-written)" where relevant, and their claims about rivals should be treated as biased. Third-party price figures for quote-only products mostly trace back to Vendr transaction data, not to vendor list prices.

## 1. Stakeholder / government-affairs software (Quorum, FiscalNote, Borealis, Darzin, Jambo, Simply Stakeholders)

### Takeaway
Two distinct product families. (a) US public-affairs suites (Quorum, FiscalNote/PolicyNote, Bloomberg Government) cost roughly US$7.5k–24k+/yr, are quote-only, and are built around legislative tracking plus a stakeholder CRM. (b) Community/ESG "stakeholder engagement" CRMs (Borealis, Jambo, Simply Stakeholders/Darzin, Tractivity) target mining, infrastructure and public-consultation teams at ~US$12k–24k+/yr. Neither family researches *who* the stakeholders are for a new foreign market; both assume you already know them and need to log interactions. FiscalNote is shrinking: delisted from NYSE (March 2026), 25% headcount cut, serial divestitures, ongoing strategic review.

### Inferences
- All of these are systems of record for relationships you already have. None generates a sourced map of unknown foreign stakeholders, which is Portolan's core job. Portolan is closer to a "first dossier" product that could feed a CRM later.
- Worth learning: Simply Stakeholders' "relationship health score" and network visualisation, Jambo's commitments/grievances log, and Quorum's meeting-prep and business-card capture all map onto Portolan's tracker and debrief features. Buyers in this category expect an interaction timeline per stakeholder.
- Don't copy: the onboarding-fee-plus-annual-seat model and 3-month implementation (Quorum per G2). At US$1k–4k per dossier, Portolan has to deliver value in days with no setup.
- FiscalNote's shift to MCP/API delivery, and its Asia alliance, suggest incumbents are moving policy data into AI agents. A Claude-based product may end up competing with, or plugging into, PolicyNote MCP for US legislative data.

### Gaps
- No public pricing for Simply Stakeholders, Darzin or Borealis tiers beyond G-Cloud; the "$2,000/month" claim is from a competitor and isn't confirmed.
- I could not independently confirm the Irth Solutions acquisition of Borealis (only the competitor Tractivity reports it).
- No evidence that any of these tools covers Taiwan government stakeholders specifically.

## 2. Procurement / government-contract intelligence (GovWin IQ, GovSpend, HigherGov, GovTribe, BGOV, TED tools, TendersInfo, Govini)

### Takeaway
US GovCon intelligence has split into cheap self-serve tools with published prices (HigherGov US$500–2,500/yr, GovTribe ~US$1.2k–2.1k/seat) and quote-only incumbents (GovWin IQ about US$6k–42k+, GovSpend median about US$11.6k, BGOV about US$6k–15k/seat). Everyone added AI and MCP in 2025–26. Coverage is almost entirely US federal/SLED (plus Canada for GovWin, and EU/NATO for a few). Global tender aggregators (TendersInfo, Tender Impulse, BiddingSource) are cheap (US$60–750/yr) but only list notices. Govini (now "Air") sells to DoD, not to vendors. None of them maps the human stakeholders behind a Taiwan MND or US DoD drone buy.

### Inferences
- The buyer side (Govini/Air, Hazel) and the vendor side (GovWin, HigherGov, GovTribe) are both well served in the US. A first-time foreign entrant still has to work out who decides, why, and what to do next. These tools answer "what is being bought, when, and who won before".
- Portolan should **integrate or cite** cheap US data (SAM.gov is free; HigherGov has an MCP server) rather than rebuild opportunity search. For Taiwan, no comparable English-language aggregator turned up beyond generic global tender sites.
- Learn from GovTribe/HigherGov: publish prices, offer a self-serve trial, and expose an MCP/API. Fit scores and PWin are now table stakes, so Portolan's influence/support scores must show their working (they already link to sources) to stand apart.
- Don't copy: GovWin-style opaque quotes and auto-renewals; that's the friction cheaper rivals advertise against.

### Gaps
- No Taiwan-specific procurement intelligence product found. I did not verify access or English support for Taiwan's Government e-Procurement System (PCC), or whether aggregators cover Taiwan MND tenders well.
- GovTribe's current plan structure conflicts across sources (per-seat vs Launch/Growth tiers).
- No verified reviews (G2/Capterra) were collected for GovWin, GovSpend or HigherGov in this pass.

## 3. Market-entry / export platforms, trade data and productised market-entry reports

### Takeaway
Trade-data and B2B directory tools (ImportGenius, Panjiva, Export Genius, Tradewheel, Kompass) tell you who ships what. They are useful for finding distributors, but irrelevant to government/defence buyers. Productised market-entry reports are a crowded, low-priced niche: from US$200–749 (AI/freelance/student), through US$1,950–5,000 (boutique fixed-fee), to US$12k–15k+ (6-week go/no-go studies). The US Commercial Service (US$2,500–3,250) is the most credible human-matchmaking benchmark, and its SME discounts were removed in July 2026. Syndicated data (Statista US$199–649/month; Euromonitor £15k–250k/yr) is generic and restricts publication rights.

### Inferences
- **Pricing anchor for Portolan:** the US$1,000 pilot sits between the AI/freelance tier (US$200–749) and boutique fixed-fee reports (US$1,950–3,450). The US$4,000+ dossier lines up with GIA (US$3,450), Market Path (US$5,000) and the Gold Key (US$3,250). The price is defensible only if the output includes named, sourced stakeholders and a tracker, which most of these reports lack.
- "Every claim sourced" is **no longer unique**: GIA (confidence level per claim), Foragentis ("every claim traces to a named source") and DiligenceSquared (section 4) all advertise it. Admiralty grading plus check dates plus the "unverified" label is a sharper version of the same claim, and Portolan should show it on screen, not just say it.
- Learn from GIA: be explicit about where a desk report stops (the fieldwork questions list) and offer the next rung (audit/pilot). Learn from the Gold Key: buyers pay for *meetings with interested parties*, not for reports. Portolan's human-delivered intros match that.
- Don't copy: syndicated "same report for every buyer" (Statista/Euromonitor), or low-end template reports with fake urgency counters (TheSoftSolution).
- The removal of SME discounts at the US Commercial Service (July 2026) widens the price gap a US$1k pilot could fill for US startups going abroad. It doesn't help Taiwanese or other non-US firms entering the USA.

### Gaps
- Kompass pricing was not retrieved.
- Big-consultancy (e.g., Big 4, Frost & Sullivan) market-entry report prices weren't found in public sources.
- No Taiwan-government equivalent fees (TAITRA, or Taiwan's Department of Investment Services) were collected; I also found no productised "Taiwan drone market entry report" with a public price.
- Janes and Forecast International subscription prices are not public.

## 4. AI research tools (deep research, Perplexity, AlphaSense, Hebbia, Crayon/Klue, AI-native GTM/market-entry startups)

### Takeaway
General deep-research agents are cheap (US$17–500/month; Parallel API US$0.10–2.40 per task) but measurably unreliable on citations: 3–13% of URLs are fabricated, and factual accuracy against the cited source is only 39–77% for frontier models, getting *worse* with more searches. Enterprise research and CI tools (AlphaSense, Klue, Crayon) cost about US$18k–30k+/yr and target public-company or SaaS competitor research, not foreign government stakeholders. In 2025–26 a wave of YC companies (Usul, Locke, Capto/Chromie, Candor, GovEagle, DiligenceSquared) attacked government GTM and "auditable McKinsey reports". They are almost all US-government-focused.

### Cited Findings
**Prices of general deep-research tools**
- ChatGPT Plus US$20; Pro US$100/200/500; Claude Pro US$20 (US$17 annual), Max US$100, Ultra US$200; Google AI Pro US$19.99, Ultra US$99.99/199.99; Perplexity Pro US$17/month annual, Max US$200 (US$167 annual). No vendor publishes a per-report count. APIs: Parallel Task API US$0.10 (pro) to US$0.30–2.40 (ultra) per task; Gemini Deep Research agent estimated US$1–7 per task — [Hyperresearch](https://hyperresearch.ai/alternatives/deep-research-pricing) (vendor-written)

- Google AI plan prices: Plus US$4.99, Pro US$19.99, Ultra 5x US$99.99, Ultra 20x US$199.99 — [Google One](https://one.google.com/about/google-ai-plans/)

**Known weaknesses: hallucination and stale data**
- Across 10 models/agents (DRBench, 53,090 URLs), 3–13% of citation URLs were hallucinated and 5–18% didn't resolve. Deep research agents hallucinate *more* (10.7% pooled) than search-augmented LLMs (4.8%). Gemini 2.5 Pro Deep Research had the highest rate at 13.3%; OpenAI Deep Research 3.5% fabricated but 10.1% non-resolving (mostly stale). A URL liveness tool cut non-resolving citations to under 1% — [arXiv 2604.03173](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03173)
- Frontier models keep link validity above 94% but only 39–77% factual accuracy against the cited source. Accuracy fell about 42% as tool calls rose from 2 to 150 (GPT-5.4: 79%→17%; Claude Opus 4.6: 80%→58%) — [arXiv 2605.06635](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.06635)
- In one open-source DR system (AI-Q), 84.7% of final-report errors came from the orchestrator agent, about 31% of them hallucinations and the rest citation mistakes — [EMNLP 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.1681.pdf)
- When asked about 40 *fictional* entities, Perplexity produced bad sources in 100% of first-citation-request runs and ChatGPT in 15.7%; "SAFE" prompt framing eliminated fabrication in ChatGPT (pilot study, Zenodo preprint) — [Varga 2026](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.20509754)
- Bibliographic test (400 references): only 26.5% fully accurate and 39.8% flawed or fabricated; Copilot, Perplexity and Claude had the highest fabrication shares — [JDIS 2026](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/jdis-2025-0326/html)
- No studies were found specifically on hallucinated *people/contacts* in stakeholder research (see Gaps).

**AlphaSense / Hebbia / Klue / Crayon**
- AlphaSense: quote-only; Vendr median US$18,375 (range about US$9,250–51,000); reported 5–10 seat minimum; expert calls add 20–40% — [Market Intelligence Tools](https://marketintelligencetools.com/reports/competitive-intelligence-pricing/); SpendHound: SMB average US$10,131/yr, enterprise US$139,455/yr (160 customers) — [SpendHound](https://www.spendhound.com/marketplace/alphasense-pricing); content is SEC filings, transcripts, broker research and expert-call libraries (Tegus) — [Tomba](https://tomba.io/blog/alphasense-pricing-reviews-pros-and-cons)

G2: Klue 4.7 (444 reviews), Crayon 4.6 (385); Klue complaints are depth, alert noise and overload; Crayon complaints include mobile, integrations, and "AI features too little too late". Klue has an MCP server in Claude's connector directory — [Linkeddit](https://linkeddit.com/blog/crayon-vs-klue); [Calven](https://calven.ai/resources/comparisons/klue-pricing) (both vendor-written)

### Inferences
- The published evidence directly supports Portolan's product rules (a source for every claim, "unverified" labels, check dates). Fabricated and stale URLs are a documented, measurable failure of off-the-shelf deep research, and URL-liveness checking fixes most of it. Portolan should run automated link-liveness and excerpt-match checks and show the results. That's a concrete edge over a US$20 ChatGPT report.
- The finding that accuracy falls as search depth rises argues for narrow, per-stakeholder research tasks over one giant agent run.
- Closest direct competitors are **Usul** (decision-maker mapping for defence, multi-country) and **Locke/Capto** (AI + human, through to the contract). All are US-government-centric, and none advertises Taiwan, cross-border entry, or team-network warm intros.
- YC's own RFS describing stakeholder mapping plus next-best-action plus deal tracking is both validation and a warning: expect more well-funded entrants in exactly this shape.
- Don't copy: Klue/Crayon-style alert noise and information overload (top reviewer complaints). Portolan's single next step per stakeholder and its one-page A4 report are the opposite design, and that's good.

### Gaps
- Hebbia pricing and features weren't retrieved.
- No pricing found for Usul, Locke or Capto.
- No benchmark specifically measures fabricated *person names or job titles* in AI stakeholder research; the evidence is about citation URLs and references.
- Product Hunt 2025–26 launches weren't searched separately.

## 5. Sales-intelligence tools for buying committees (Sales Navigator, Apollo, ZoomInfo, Clay)

### Takeaway
These tools are cheap per seat (Sales Navigator US$90–160/month, Apollo US$49–119/seat/month, Clay US$167–495/month; ZoomInfo is quote-only, about US$15k+/yr) and good at private-sector, North American decision makers. They are weak where Portolan operates: government and military officials, Asian (incl. Taiwan) non-English organisations, and verification. Sales Navigator gives no emails; Apollo email accuracy is reported at about 65–80%; coverage drops outside North America.

### Cited Findings
- Sales Navigator: Core US$119.99/month or US$1,079.88/yr; Advanced US$159.99/month or US$1,799.88/yr (self-serve capped at 5 licences); Advanced Plus quote-only (the only tier with CRM sync); 50 InMails/month; no emails or phone numbers — [Needle](https://useneedle.net/comparisons/apollo-vs-clay-vs-sales-navigator); same figures in [ZoomInfo blog](https://pipeline.zoominfo.com/sales/top-sales-intelligence-tools)
- Sales Navigator "TeamLink" shows warm introduction paths through your team's network; search is English-only per one APAC data vendor (vendor-written) — [Pintel](https://pintel.ai/blogs/best-b2b-apac-company-data-providers-sales/)
- Apollo: Free (75 credits/month); Basic US$49/seat/month annual; Professional US$79; Organization US$119 (3-seat minimum); 1 credit per email, 8 per phone; credits expire — [Needle](https://useneedle.net/comparisons/apollo-vs-clay-vs-sales-navigator); 240M contacts with no regional split published — [LeadOcean](https://leadocean.io/blog/best-b2b-data-providers-apac)
- Clay: Free; Launch US$167/month annual; Growth US$446/month annual (CRM/webhooks/API); a waterfall over 150+ providers and the "Claygent" web research agent; Clay has no verification layer of its own — [Needle](https://useneedle.net/comparisons/apollo-vs-clay-vs-sales-navigator); [ZoomInfo vs Clay](https://www.zoominfo.com/compare/clay-vs-zoominfo) (vendor-written)
- ZoomInfo: quote-only (pay-as-you-go GTM AI from US$20, US$0.25–0.45 per credit); claims 500M contacts, 100M companies, 300+ human researchers; Asia 4.8M companies, 68.3M contacts, 12.9M mobiles (no per-country table); official MCP server — [LeadOcean](https://leadocean.io/blog/best-b2b-data-providers-apac); [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/compare/clay-vs-zoominfo)
- Independent testing puts Apollo email accuracy at about 65–80%; "most databases are strongest in North America and weaker in EMEA, APAC, and LATAM"; ZoomInfo is "patchy outside North America" — [Alex Berman](https://alexberman.com/b2b-lead-database-tools)
- Japan example: ZoomInfo covers large listed firms but Japan SME coverage is "thin"; Apollo coverage drops for "Japanese-language-only companies"; ZoomInfo is US$15k+/yr enterprise — [SyncGTM](https://syncgtm.com/blog/best-b2b-database-japan) (vendor-written)
- Intent tools work at the account level, so "you know a company is 'in-market' but have no idea who's actually driving the buying decision" (vendor-written) — [Precept](https://www.preceptai.co.uk/blog/precept-vs-zoominfo-apollo-clay-cognism-sales-navigator)

### Inferences
- Buying-committee tools assume a private-company buyer with LinkedIn-visible staff. Taiwan MND, NCSIST and US DoD program offices are poorly represented, and many relevant officials are deliberately not on LinkedIn. That's the gap a sourced, public-record-based stakeholder map fills.
- TeamLink is the closest existing "who knows whom" feature. Portolan's upload-your-LinkedIn-export model gives similar warm-path insight without a LinkedIn API, and works across team members who don't all pay for Sales Navigator.
- Clay's "no verification layer of its own" is an opening: Portolan's per-claim source plus "unverified" label is the verification layer.
- Don't copy: credit-metered contact reveals or bulk email sequencing. Portolan's rules say no auto-sending messages, and buying contact data for government officials creates privacy and credibility risk.

### Gaps
- No quantitative coverage data for Taiwan specifically, or for government/military employees in any of these databases.
- LinkedIn's current terms on data export and scraping weren't retrieved in this pass. The legal basis for processing user-uploaded LinkedIn connection exports should be checked separately.

## 6. Which products combine research + human expert + follow-through, and where is the gap?

### Takeaway
Research + human + outcome tracking exists only in two forms: expensive human services (defence GTM consultancies US$20–50k/month or US$40–120k per engagement; Foremark US$15k→75k; Vantage US$12–18k plus retainer), and new US-only AI-plus-human firms (Locke, Capto, Clipper Defense). Low-cost options are either data-only (GovCon tools, sales intel, trade data) or report-only (US$500–3,450 fixed-fee reports, with no tracker or follow-up). The open slot: a sub-US$5k, auditable (sourced, graded, dated) stakeholder dossier for a *specific foreign government/defence market* (Taiwan↔USA drones), combined with the team's own warm paths, a tracker/debrief loop, and optional human review or intros.

### Cited Findings
- Locke: AI agents plus in-house lobbyists "own the entire path - from research to first meeting to funded contract" (US government only) — [YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/SSp-locke-the-first-ai-government-affairs-firm)
- Capto: "AI-native software with hands-on expertise", sold as managed services or SaaS (US government) — [YC GovTech](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/GovTech)
- Clipper Defense: pairs startups with "a dedicated defense expert from our BD network". Case study: an autonomy startup went to a full GTM plan in 90 days, got a Tradewinds listing in 46 days, and 9+ defence VC intros — [Clipper Defense case](https://www.clipperdefense.com/case-studies/autonomous-vehicle-startup-gtm); deliverables include TAM/SAM/SOM "with cited sources" and a 24–36 month roadmap — [Clipper Defense](https://www.clipperdefense.com/services/strategy)
- Winston Francois (agency): A&D GTM engagements US$50K–120K over 3–6 months plus US$10–20K/month retainers; another page says US$20K–50K/month; deliverables include "relationship mapping systems" that track program managers and contracting officers (agency marketing; the two pages give inconsistent prices) — [WF A&D GTM](https://winstonfrancois.com/blog/gtm-strategy-for-aerospace-defense/); [WF go-to-market A&D](https://winstonfrancois.com/blog/go-to-market-for-aerospace-defense/)
- Marcantonio Global: free capability assessment, then "Signal" at US$2,500/month; covers SBIR→Phase III/OTA/DIU pathways and stakeholder engagement with OSD/COCOMs — [Marcantonio Global](https://www.marcantonioglobal.com/dtiaas)
- US Commercial Service Gold Key (US$3,250) is human matchmaking with up to 5 interested partners, staff attending, and a written report, but there's no ongoing tracking and it's US exporters only — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/gold-key-service)
- Tradewheel's top tiers bundle "Monthly Progress Reporting", pre-visit meetings and negotiation follow-up at US$3,999–7,999/yr (B2B goods, not government) — [Tradewheel](https://www.tradewheel.com/premium-services/)
- Jambo/Quorum/Simply Stakeholders provide tracking (interactions, commitments, health scores) but no research — see section 1 sources.
- GIA explicitly sells a ladder: report (US$3,450), then audit, then 8–12 week pilot with real buyers — [GIA](https://go-international-advisory.com/services/market-intelligence-report.html)
- Live Taiwan context: Taiwan is positioning itself as Asia's "non-red" drone supply-chain hub (VP Hsiao, Oct 2026), and China's revised UAV export controls take effect 18 Nov 2026 — [Janes](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/air/taiwan-eyes-to-emerge-as-asias-drone-supply-chain-hub); Taiwanese drone tenders have been delayed, and Coretronic's CIRC partnered with US firm Aevex to pursue them (Oct 2026) — [DIGITIMES](https://www.digitimes.com/news/a20261029PD230/circ-coretronic-partnership-government-subsidiary.html)

### Inferences
- **Gap 1: geography.** Almost every AI GovCon and government-affairs product found is US-federal-first. The Taiwan market (MND, NCSIST, MOEA drone programmes, GPL Article 104 rules) and the reverse direction (Taiwanese drone firms entering the US) have no productised, sourced stakeholder tool. Janes and FI cover budgets and programmes, not people or next steps.
- **Gap 2: price point.** Human-led defence GTM runs US$20k+/month; AI reports cost US$500–3,450 but end at the PDF. Portolan's US$1k pilot / US$4k dossier sits where nobody offers both a dossier and follow-through.
- **Gap 3: auditability.** Admiralty-style source grading plus check dates plus an explicit "unverified" label is rare. Only GIA's "confidence level on every claim" comes close, and the academic evidence (section 4) shows generic deep research fails exactly here.
- **Gap 4: warm paths.** Only Sales Navigator TeamLink offers team-network intro paths, and it doesn't work for officials who aren't on LinkedIn or for non-English profiles.
- Biggest competitive threats: Usul (multi-country decision-maker mapping for defence), Locke and Capto (well-funded AI-plus-human, outcome-owning), and incumbents adding MCP/AI (FiscalNote PolicyNote MCP, HigherGov MCP, Klue MCP). Portolan could *use* some of these as data sources rather than fight them.
- What Portolan must not copy: lobbying or "we own the outcome" claims (legal and registration exposure, e.g., US lobbying disclosure and foreign-agent rules; not researched here); auto-sending outreach; opaque quote-only pricing; sold contact data on government officials.

### Gaps
- No public prices for Usul, Locke, Capto, Clipper Defense or FLT Consulting.
- No public outcome data (win rates) for any AI-plus-human GovCon firm; Locke's US$500K run-rate is self-reported.
- Didn't research legal constraints (FARA/LDA in the US; Taiwan lobbying law) on human-intro services that touch government officials. Relevant to Portolan's expert-intro service.
- No Taiwan-based competitor (e.g., a Taiwanese consultancy selling drone market-entry dossiers to foreign firms) was identified. That may be a search-coverage gap, since searches were English-only.
