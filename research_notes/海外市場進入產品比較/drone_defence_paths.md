# How drone / defence-tech startups win a first contract in Taiwan and the US (both directions), and who helps them

Research date: 2026-11-04. All items dated; "⚠" marks uncertain, conflicting or likely-superseded items. Figures are as reported by the cited source; NT$/US$ conversions are the source's own.

## Q1. Taiwan: how foreign (US/European) drone and counter-UAS firms actually sell into Taiwan — channels, rules, local partner, budgets, non-red rules

### Takeaway
Foreign firms almost never win Taiwanese drone/C-UAS money directly: the big domestic tenders (MND Armaments Bureau, Coast Guard, National Police Agency) are restricted to Taiwan-registered bidders with China-free supply chains, so the real path is (a) FMS/DCS for lethal or major systems, or (b) partnering with a Taiwanese prime/integrator (AIDC, NCSIST, Getac, Thunder Tiger, etc.) as technology/software supplier, licensor or co-producer. Demand is huge on paper (48,750 MND drones for 2026–27; ~210,000 more planned), but the special budget was gutted in May 2026 and replaced by a six-year NT$240 billion annual-budget framework (passed 27 Aug 2026), so large new domestic awards are likely to slip to mid-2027.

### Cited Findings

**Legal channels and thresholds**
- Military procurement is governed by Article 104 of Taiwan's Government Procurement Law (GPL), which defines three forms: domestic tenders, Foreign Military Sales (FMS) and Direct Commercial Sales (DCS); US firms can sell via FMS or DCS. — [trade.gov CCG Taiwan – Defense](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/taiwan-defense)
- Military purchases above NT$30 million (~US$1M) locally or NT$18.5 million (~US$0.6M) from outside Taiwan must be procured through the MND. — [trade.gov CCG Taiwan – Defense](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/taiwan-defense); same thresholds in [trade.gov CCG – Highly Automated and Autonomous Systems (Nov 2025)](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/taiwan-highly-automated-and-autonomous-systems)
- The Legislative Yuan must be notified of FMS tenders above US$50 million total, or US$14 million for major defence equipment. — [trade.gov CCG Taiwan – Defense](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/taiwan-defense)
- GPL exceptions exist for weapons/national-security procurement in emergencies, confidential procurement, wartime/mobilisation, or single-supplier cases. — [trade.gov CCG Taiwan – Defense](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/taiwan-defense)
- Defence procurement mix (older baseline, 2017–2019): ~7,200–7,700 domestic tender cases/yr vs 27–40 DCS and 24–33 FMS cases/yr, per the Armaments Bureau. ⚠ dated 2019. — [trade.gov CCG Taiwan – Aerospace and Defense (2019)](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/taiwan-aerospace-and-defense-0)
- "Most foreign firms gain their initial foothold in the Taiwan market by appointing a local agent." ⚠ general (non-defence) guidance, 2019. — [trade.gov CCG – Distribution and Sales Channels](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/taiwan-distribution-and-sales-channels)
- ITA describes hurdles for US new entrants in Taiwan defence: "finding local partners, navigating offset requirements and technology transfer, and growing competition from both domestic and international manufacturers." — [ITA Taiwan UAS & C-UAS Business Development Mission page](https://www.trade.gov/aerospace-defense-trade-mission-taiwan)

**MND / Armaments Bureau drone tenders (domestic-only)**
- First "military-use commercial-grade" UAV tender (announced 2022): 3,422 systems across six ISR types, ~NT$6.9 billion. — [DSET Taiwan's Drone Dome report (Aug/Sep 2026)](https://dset.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/26-08-31_%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A_%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E7%84%A1%E4%BA%BA%E6%A9%9F_%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E7%89%88-1.pdf)
- Winners of that first tender: Evergreen Aviation Technologies (ship-based ISR), 智飛科技 (land ISR), Coretronic Intelligent Robotics (micro + ISR), Mitac/神通 (micro target acquisition + tactical); total ~NT$6.887 billion; a second contract went to the same four in Aug 2025. — [TechNews (23 Jan 2026)](https://technews.tw/2026/01/23/1250-billion-budget-creates-chances-and-challenges-to-taiwanese-manufacturers/)
- Second tender: Armaments Bureau notice on the e-Procurement System (22 Jul 2025) for 48,750 drones over 2026–2027, five types A–E; the bureau's Material Production and Manufacturing Center invited **domestic** manufacturers to submit models. — [Taipei Times (24 Jul 2025)](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/front/archives/2025/07/24/2003840815)
- Split: 11,270 units in 2026 and 37,480 in 2027. Type A multirotor (>6 km) 7,500 + 26,500; Type B multirotor (>25 km) 1,100 + 3,200; Type C fixed-wing (>90 km, 2 h) 970 + 2,980; Type D fixed-wing (>30 km) 1,350 + 4,450; Type E VTOL fixed-wing (>100 km, 2.5 h) 350 + 350. All must be manufactured and assembled in Taiwan; Chinese components prohibited. — [trade.gov CCG – Highly Automated and Autonomous Systems](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/taiwan-highly-automated-and-autonomous-systems); confirmed in [Taipei Times (28 Jun 2026)](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/06/28/2003859868)
- Value ~NT$50 billion; Chinese firms and Chinese-capital firms barred; module components must be made in Taiwan, but **foreign suppliers may be used for technologies Taiwan lacks, e.g. flight-control software and satellite-communication chips**. FPV is the largest category (up to 34,000). — [Taiwan News (CNA)](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6253355)
- As of June 2026 the 48,750 buy was being funded "through regular government operations or related budget allocations already funded", separate from the special budget. — [Taipei Times (28 Jun 2026)](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/06/28/2003859868)

**Special defence budget saga (status as of Nov 2026)**
- Nov 2025: Executive Yuan proposed a NT$1.25 trillion (US$40.4B) special defence budget including ~210,000 domestically produced UAVs and 1,320 USVs. May 2026: opposition-controlled legislature removed these items entirely; the NT$780 billion version enacted was "FMS only; domestic UAV/USV and DCS programs removed". — [DSET Drone Dome report](https://dset.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/26-08-31_%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A_%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E7%84%A1%E4%BA%BA%E6%A9%9F_%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E7%89%88-1.pdf)
- June 2026: successor NT$210 billion special bill for 210,966 drones (208,200 littoral attack drones, 1,446 littoral recon drones, 1,320 small suicide USVs) by end-2031. — [Taipei Times (28 Jun 2026)](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2026/06/28/2003859868)
- 27 Aug 2026: legislature passed an opposition-led version — a six-year NT$240 billion **annual-budget** framework keeping three MND categories (coastal-surveillance UAVs, coastal-attack UAVs, small one-way-attack USVs); funding depends on annual appropriations; DSET expects earliest procurement mid-2027 and possibly no large domestic combat-UAV contracts in 2026. — [DSET Drone Dome report](https://dset.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/26-08-31_%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A_%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E7%84%A1%E4%BA%BA%E6%A9%9F_%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E7%89%88-1.pdf); [DSET press release (2 Sep 2026)](https://dset.tw/en/publication-en/000472-2/)
- AFP (3 Nov 2026): parliament "recently passed a NT$240 billion (US$7.4 billion) drone defence bill, with more spending expected to be approved this month." — [AFP](https://www.afp.com/en/sea-drone-makers-compete-taiwan-soaring-investment)
- Approved FMS within the enacted special budget: 2,032 ALTIUS-600 ISR / ALTIUS-700M systems (~US$1.1B). Switchblade 300 contract: 685 systems ~NT$1.74B (~US$79k per munition, package basis). — [DSET report](https://dset.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/26-08-31_%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A_%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E7%84%A1%E4%BA%BA%E6%A9%9F_%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E7%89%88-1.pdf); [DSET newsletter (20 Aug 2026)](https://dset.tw/en/research/000461-2/)
- Civilian/interagency plan: 50,898 drones, ~NT$10 billion, delivery by 2028. — [DSET report](https://dset.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/26-08-31_%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A_%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E7%84%A1%E4%BA%BA%E6%A9%9F_%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E7%89%88-1.pdf)
- Industry programme: Executive Yuan approved the NT$44.2 billion Unmanned Vehicle Industry Development Integrated Program (Oct 2025). — [DSET report](https://dset.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/26-08-31_%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A_%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E7%84%A1%E4%BA%BA%E6%A9%9F_%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E7%89%88-1.pdf)

**Non-red supply chain requirements**
- Government goal: fully non-PRC ("non-red") drone supply chain by 2027, reaffirmed at a Jan 2026 Executive Yuan meeting. — [DSET report](https://dset.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/26-08-31_%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A_%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E7%84%A1%E4%BA%BA%E6%A9%9F_%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E7%89%88-1.pdf)
- ⚠ DSET lists a "four-tier A–D non-red sourcing framework introduced in January 2027" in a report dated Aug/Sep 2026 — the date is probably a typo (likely Jan 2026); verify before use. — [DSET report](https://dset.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/26-08-31_%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A_%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E7%84%A1%E4%BA%BA%E6%A9%9F_%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E7%89%88-1.pdf)
- Vice President Hsiao (late Oct 2026): Taiwan "committed to providing a 100% non-red supply chain" covering microprocessors, flight controllers, optical payloads, batteries; China's revised UAV export controls take effect 18 Nov 2026. Remaining PRC dependence: batteries, comms modules, flight control, navigation, software (per DSET). — [Janes (3 Nov 2026)](https://www.janes.com/defence-intelligence-insights/defence-news/air/taiwan-eyes-to-emerge-as-asias-drone-supply-chain-hub)
- Typical tender clause (Coast Guard medium-range drone, CGA115014): bars PRC/HK/Macau firms and any firm with such capital; bidders must sign a declaration and list nationality of representatives, directors, supervisors, managers and every >10% shareholder; missing form = disqualified. — [tenderrader mirror of PCC notice (29 Oct 2026)](https://tenderrader.com/t/A.47.1/CGA115014)

**Coast Guard (CGA) — the clearest live example of the foreign-OEM + Taiwan-bidder model**
- Shipborne VTOL drone tender (NT$1.2B, 6 sets/12 aircraft): CGA allowed **only domestic bidders**, so Shield AI (V-BAT) bid via AIDC and Airbus (Flexrotor) via Getac (神基). First opening on 17 Jun 2026 failed because only two bids were received (<3 required by GPA for first round); second opening 25 Jun (one bid suffices), POC flight test in July. — [Upmedia (18 Jun 2026)](https://www.upmedia.mg/tw/investigation/military/261610)
- AIDC/Shield AI won the NT$1.2 billion contract for 12 V-BATs in July 2026; Shield AI provides flight software, AIDC other support. — [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6425839); first V-BAT delivered 13 Oct 2026 per [FlightBrief (13 Oct 2026)](https://flightbrief.news/articles/taiwan-coast-guard-reopens-medium-range-drone-tender/)
- Medium-range drone tender NT$782.7 million: first round (opened May) declared void 11 Aug 2026; reopened 13 Oct with rewritten spec; NT$35 million bid bond; price ≥20% of score; most-advantageous-tender award; not covered by WTO GPA (national-security exclusion); bids in Traditional Chinese (English allowed for technical material). ⚠ Bid deadline conflict: FlightBrief says bids close 9 Nov / open 10 Nov; the corrected PCC notice (29 Oct) says deadline 25 Nov 2026 17:00, opening 26 Nov. — [FlightBrief](https://flightbrief.news/articles/taiwan-coast-guard-reopens-medium-range-drone-tender/); [tenderrader (PCC mirror)](https://tenderrader.com/t/A.47.1/CGA115014)
- CGA plan overall: ~NT$2.1 billion for 451 VTOL drones (412 short-range, 18 medium-range, 12 shipborne, 9 special task unit). — [FlightBrief](https://flightbrief.news/articles/taiwan-coast-guard-reopens-medium-range-drone-tender/)
- Florida-based Maritime Tactical Systems: CGA to buy 25 of its USVs, made in the US and Taiwan. — [AFP (3 Nov 2026)](https://www.afp.com/en/sea-drone-makers-compete-taiwan-soaring-investment)

**National Police Agency and Army counter-UAS**
- NPA: NT$3.67 billion for 50 regional soft-kill C-UAS systems for critical infrastructure (16 Tier A at NT$85M, 10 Tier B at NT$75M, 24 Tier C at NT$65M), one systems integrator per set, ≥5 km radius, radar + RF detection + jamming + C2. **Bidding restricted to Taiwanese entities**; key components and comms modules must not be Chinese-made. — [DSET newsletter (20 Aug 2026)](https://dset.tw/en/research/000461-2/)
- Army fixed-site C-UAS (~NT$989M, 26 systems over 3 years): awarded to Tron Future Tech in 2024; systems failed three rounds of acceptance testing; MND initiated termination; possible re-tender 2027. Control Yuan (Apr 2026) found deficiencies; handheld jammers failed in the 2025 Han Kuang exercise. — [DSET newsletter (20 Aug 2026)](https://dset.tw/en/research/000461-2/)

**NCSIST — the main door for foreign technology**
- Auterion–NCSIST MoU (17 Jun 2025): described as NCSIST's first international agreement with a defence-tech company; AuterionOS and Nemesis swarming to be integrated into NCSIST and Taiwanese UxVs. — [Auterion](https://auterion.com/auterion-and-taiwans-ncsist-announce-strategic-partnership-to-power-next-generation-defense-drones/)
- At TADTE (Sept 2025) NCSIST signed contracts/MoUs with six foreign firms: Airshare (Canada; C-UAS rocket), Anduril, Leonardo DRS, AeroVironment, MARTAC, Northrop Grumman — all emphasising local production and technology transfer. — [Taipei Times (20 Sep 2025)](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/taiwan/archives/2025/09/20/2003844127)
- Shield AI–NCSIST contract (11 Feb 2026) to integrate Hivemind into NCSIST systems; swarm demo completed Aug 2026 and triggered expansion. Kratos co-developing Mighty Hornet IV; Anduril Lattice being evaluated with Taiwan's Rui Zhi C2. NCSIST has partnered with nine foreign firms on software. — [Shield AI](https://shield.ai/shield-ai-signs-contract-with-taiwans-national-chung-shan-institute-of-science-and-technology-to-accelerate-and-indigenize-taiwan-developed-ai-pilots/); [DSET newsletter (20 Aug 2026)](https://dset.tw/en/research/000461-2/); [DSET press release](https://dset.tw/en/publication-en/000472-2/)
- Industry-to-industry: Thunder Tiger licensed Auterion AI platforms; Ubiqconn–AeroVironment collaboration on unified drone control. — [Defence Innovation Review (19 Jun 2026)](https://defenceinnovationreview.com/2026/06/19/taiwans-drone-surge-reshapes-global-defence-market/)
- What Taiwanese industry wants from US firms: licensing, co-design, co-production in IR cameras, long-range comms modules, military radio, flight-control software. — [trade.gov CCG – Defense](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/taiwan-defense)

### Inferences
- For a US/European startup, the "first contract" in Taiwan is realistically a **teaming/subcontract or licence with a Taiwanese bidder** (AIDC, Getac, Thunder Tiger, Coretronic etc.) or an **NCSIST MoU → software/integration contract**, not a direct government award. Portolan's stakeholder map should model the Taiwanese prime as the gatekeeper, with the end agency behind it.
- Software/autonomy, comms and sensor companies have the clearest opening (explicitly exempted from the "made in Taiwan" component rule); airframe makers mainly compete via co-production.
- The domestic-only, three-bidder rule, Chinese-language tender docs, large bid bonds (NT$35M) and frequent re-tenders mean timing and partner choice matter more than product.
- Budget risk is high: the political deadlock means the briefs must clearly separate "funded and tendering now" (48,750 MND buy, CGA, NPA) from "authorised but not appropriated" (NT$240B framework).

## Q2. Taiwan helpers: TEDIBOA, Asia UAV AI Innovation Application R&D Center, TAITRA, AIT, consultancies, trade shows

### Takeaway
Helpers in Taiwan are mostly government-backed and face **outward** (getting Taiwanese makers abroad), not inward (helping foreign startups win Taiwanese tenders). For an incoming US/European firm the main official channels are AIT's Commercial Section / ITA missions and NCSIST/AIDC partnerships; TADTE (biennial) is the key show.

- **TEDIBOA** (Taiwan Excellence Drone International Business Opportunities Alliance): launched Sept 2024 by MOEA, led by AIDC with seven major companies (Mitac Advance Technology, Thunder Tiger, Coretronic, Geosat…); >100 members per trade.gov. — [trade.gov Taiwan Drone Systems](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/taiwan-drone-systems)

## Q3. US: paths for a foreign (Taiwanese) drone company to sell in the US — Blue UAS, NDAA, FCC, DIU CSO, SBIR, Replicator/DAWG/Drone Dominance, state/local, primes, US entity/CMMC

### Takeaway
Since Dec 2025 the gating item for any foreign-made drone or critical component is the **FCC Covered List**: new foreign-produced UAS/components cannot get FCC equipment authorisation unless on the DCMA Blue UAS list (exempt to 1 Jan 2028), ≥65% US-content "domestic end products", or holding a DoW/DHS **Conditional Approval** tied to an onshoring plan (no expiry). So the practical route for a Taiwanese firm is: Blue UAS (or Green UAS → Blue) listing per product + US entity/US production + a US partner/prime, then compete in DoD challenge buys (Drone Dominance Gauntlets, DIU CSOs). SBIR is effectively closed to foreign-owned companies.

### Cited Findings

**Blue UAS (owner: DCMA since 3 Dec 2025)**
- DIU formally transferred the Blue UAS Cleared List to DCMA's Unmanned Systems–Experimental Command (US-X), Palmdale, on 3 Dec 2025 (ahead of the 1 Jan 2026 plan); DIU had processed 81 unique companies; DIU remains a partner on standards/checklists. — [DIU (3 Dec 2025)](https://www.diu.mil/latest/dius-blue-uas-list-to-transition-to-dcma)
- Process: vendor submits a checklist packet to the Blue List portal, contracts a Recognized Assessor who evaluates NDAA compliance, and the assessor's report is submitted via the portal. DCMA says it is accelerating this (Jul 2026). — [DCMA (27 Jul 2026)](https://www.dcma.mil/News/Article/4533954/dcma-accelerates-blue-list-process/)
- Two lists: **Cleared List** (complete aircraft) and **Framework** (components/software); the FCC treats both as "Blue UAS Cleared List". — [FCC DA 26-22 (7 Jan 2026)](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-22A1.pdf)
- Blue UAS assesses compliance with Sec. 848 FY2020 NDAA, Sec. 817 FY2023 NDAA and the American Security Drone Act (FY24 NDAA §§1821–1832), plus cyber/pen-testing. — [FCC DA 26-22](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-22A1.pdf)
- List size: >39 drone systems and 165 components (Nov 2025) ([Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6260242)); 260 Framework components as of 29 Oct 2026 per an aggregator ⚠ ([FlightBrief](https://flightbrief.news/topics/blue-uas/)).
- Foreign makers are eligible; listing is per product; Thunder Tiger is the only Taiwanese company on the Cleared List (as of Aug 2026, aggregator). ⚠ secondary — [taiwandrones.com guide (27 Aug 2026)](https://www.taiwandrones.com/guides/blue-uas-cleared-list)
- Thunder Tiger's Overkill FPV added Sept 2025, first Taiwanese/Asian system. — [TaiwanDefence (16 Oct 2026)](https://taiwandefence.com/article/thunder-tiger-says-it-has-delivered-all-1520-drones-in-its-first-pentagon-order); [IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/taiwan-drone-supply-chain). ⚠ Focus Taiwan says "added in February" (2026) — possibly a second product; unresolved. — [Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/sci-tech/202605180014)

**FCC Covered List (Dec 2025 → Jul 2026)**
- 22 Dec 2025 (DA 25-1086): FCC added all UAS and UAS critical components **produced in a foreign country**, plus DJI/Autel per FY25 NDAA §1709, to the Covered List; blocks new equipment authorisations (needed for import/marketing/sale); previously authorised models can still be sold/used. — [FCC](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-updates-covered-list-add-certain-uas-and-uas-components); [Akin](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/fcc-adds-all-foreign-made-uas-and-uas-critical-components-to-covered-list)
- "Critical components" include data links, comms, flight controllers, GCS/controllers, navigation, sensors/cameras, batteries/BMS, motors. — [Akin](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/fcc-adds-all-foreign-made-uas-and-uas-critical-components-to-covered-list)
- Nationality of the producer is irrelevant — test is place of production. A US-built drone with foreign parts is not covered if it meets Blue UAS or Buy American. — [FCC Covered List FAQ](https://www.fcc.gov/covered-list-faqs-uas-and-uas-critical-components)
- 7 Jan 2026 (DA 26-22): exemptions for Blue UAS list items and Buy American "domestic end products" until 1 Jan 2027. — [FCC DA 26-22](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DA-26-22A1.pdf). Buy American = manufactured in US with >65% domestic component cost (2024–2028), rising to 75% from 2029. — [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-FCC-Issues-Exemptions-Clarifications-for-Sweeping-Prohibition-of-Foreign-Made-Drones)
- **21 Jul 2026 (DA 26-761)**: Blue UAS and Buy American exemptions extended to **1 Jan 2028**; termination date removed for **Conditional Approvals** (valid as long as the applicant follows its onshoring plan). FCC also proposed banning import/sale for non-USG use of foreign-made "military-grade" drones (swarming, IR) (DA 26-758; comments due 2 Sep 2026). — [FCC Fact Sheet (21 Jul 2026)](https://docs.fcc.gov/public/attachments/DOC-423277A1.pdf); [fccid.io](https://fccid.io/news/fcc-extends-uas-covered-list-exemptions-conditional-approvals-da-26-761); [Rupprecht Law](https://jrupprechtlaw.com/fcc-drone-import-marketing-ban/)
- Conditional Approval requests go to drones@fcc.gov, forwarded to DoW/DHS. — [FCC FAQ](https://www.fcc.gov/covered-list-faqs-uas-and-uas-critical-components). Example grants (24 Jul 2026): Exedy Globalparts' Ayre CX, Parallel Flight Technologies' Firefly. — [DroneLife](https://dronelife.com/2026/07/27/fcc-conditional-drone-approvals-onshoring-framework/)
- ⚠ The FCC FAQ page itself still shows both 1 Jan 2027 and 1 Jan 2028 dates (inconsistent page). — [FCC FAQ](https://www.fcc.gov/covered-list-faqs-uas-and-uas-critical-components)

### Inferences

## Q4. US-side helpers: DIU, AUVSI (Green UAS, Partnership for Drone Competitiveness), SelectUSA, Taiwan–US initiatives, accelerators/BD consultancies

### Takeaway
The best-built helper for Taiwanese firms is the **certification pipeline** (AUVSI Green UAS with ITRI as in-country assessor → Blue UAS) and the government-run delegation machine (TEDIBOA/MOEA/MOFA at XPONENTIAL, state MoUs). ## Q5. Typical time to first contract, first contract size, failure reasons, cost of entry

### Takeaway
US defence: plan for **18–36 months from first meeting to first contract** and US$50k–300k pilots, with a further 1–3-year "valley of death" before production money. The 2026 challenge-buy model is faster: Thunder Tiger went from Blue UAS listing (Sept 2025) to a completed ~US$7.6M order (Oct 2026) in about 13 months. Taiwan tenders can award within weeks once open, but re-tenders, three-bidder rules, acceptance failures and budget politics routinely add months to years.

### Cited Findings
- DoD deals can take 18–36 months from first meeting to initial contract; early pilots US$50k–300k; government payment 60–120 days; feasibility ~US$300–400k/6–9 months, prototype ~US$2–2.5M/18–24 months; feasibility→production roughly 5–7 years; per a JP Morgan analysis <16% of initial SBIR recipients reached a production contract. ⚠ opinion piece citing JP Morgan second-hand — [Israel Defense (May 2026)](https://www.israeldefense.co.il/en/node/69026)
- Only ~5% of companies go from SBIR Phase II to Phase III. — [Belfer Center (Jun 2025)](https://www.belfercenter.org/research-analysis/silicon-valley-battlefield-rewiring-defense-next-fight)
- Valley of death between pilot and programme of record "typically one to two years long for survivors". — [DAU via WARU](https://www.waru.edu/library/damag/january-february2022/valley-death); pilot funding ~US$0.5–1M and 2–3 years to POM money (2021). — [Anduril](https://www.anduril.com/news/dod-valley-of-death-closing-the-two-year-gap)
- DIU CSO: ~10 business days for solution-brief review, 60–90 days to award (goal), prototype 12–24 months. — [DIU Quick Look](https://www.secnav.navy.mil/rda/DASN-P/Shared%20Documents/DIU%20Quick%20Look%20-%20Acquisition%20.pdf)
- Drone Dominance Gauntlet 1 unit economics: avg US$5,000/drone falling toward US$2,300; smallest order 1,520 units. — [war.gov](https://www.war.gov/News/News-Stories/Article/Article/4399192/as-promised-war-department-moving-out-fast-on-drone-dominance/); [Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/is-the-pentagons-demand-signal-for-drones-working-it-depends-on-who-you-ask/)
- Green UAS: ~6–8 weeks standard ([AUVSI](https://www.auvsi.org/certification-training/green-uas/request-certification/)); remediation may require restarting the assessment ([AUVSI FAQ](https://www.auvsi.org/certification-training/green-uas/faqs/)).
- Taiwan examples: CGA shipborne tender — first opening 17 Jun 2026 failed (2 bids), second 25 Jun, POC July, award July (~1 month) ([Upmedia](https://www.upmedia.mg/tw/investigation/military/261610); [Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6425839)). Army C-UAS — 2024 award, failed three acceptance tests, terminated 2026 ([DSET](https://dset.tw/en/research/000461-2/)).
- Taiwan first-contract scale examples: CGA 12 V-BATs NT$1.2B (~US$37.7M) via AIDC ([Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6425839)); Mighty Hornet I co-production initial export lot 10–50 systems ([DSET](https://dset.tw/en/research/000461-2/)).
- Failure reasons cited: no follow-on order guarantee in Gauntlets ([Breaking Defense](https://breakingdefense.com/2026/10/is-the-pentagons-demand-signal-for-drones-working-it-depends-on-who-you-ask/)); runway planned on SaaS timelines; selling to the end user rather than procurement authority and budget holder ([Israel Defense](https://www.israeldefense.co.il/en/node/69026)); acceptance-test failure (Tron Future, [DSET](https://dset.tw/en/research/000461-2/)); budget deadlock ([DSET report](https://dset.tw/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/26-08-31_%E5%A0%B1%E5%91%8A_%E5%8F%B0%E7%81%A3%E7%84%A1%E4%BA%BA%E6%A9%9F_%E7%AC%AC%E4%BA%94%E7%89%88-1.pdf)); offsets/tech transfer and finding partners in Taiwan ([ITA](https://www.trade.gov/aerospace-defense-trade-mission-taiwan)); only 24% of Gauntlet 1 drones reached 10 km ([DefenseScoop](https://defensescoop.com/2026/09/18/drone-dominance-program-gauntlet-2-results/)).
- Cost-of-entry items with sources: Taiwan bid bond NT$35M on the CGA medium-range tender ([tenderrader](https://tenderrader.com/t/A.47.1/CGA115014)); Thunder Tiger NT$1.2B capital raise incl. NT$800M for a Chiayi plant ([Taiwan News](https://www.taiwannews.com.tw/news/6277908)); CMMC Level 2 C3PAO from Nov 2026 ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2025/09/cmmc-final-rule-key-takeaways-for-defense-contractors)); accelerator fee US$25k ([I2A](https://www.indusbridgeventures.com/i2a)).

### Inferences
- For Portolan, a realistic "first win" metric differs by direction: in the US, a Blue/Green listing, CSO pitch invite, or a Gauntlet slot via a US partner; in Taiwan, a teaming agreement with the domestic bidder or an NCSIST MoU.

## Q6. What a startup BD lead needs before the first meeting, and where they get it now

### Takeaway
Before a first meeting the BD lead needs: (1) who actually holds the money and signs (agency office + Taiwanese bidder/US prime), (2) which budget line and its legislative status, (3) live/upcoming tenders with deadlines and eligibility clauses, (4) certification status required (Blue/Green UAS, FCC exemption, CMMC, non-red declarations), and (5) competitors and incumbents. Today this is scattered across Chinese-language PCC notices, think-tank newsletters, trade press, law-firm alerts and government portals that often contradict each other on dates.

### Inferences
- A brief that pulls together for one target account the gatekeeper office, the funded budget line with its legislative status, open/expected tenders with eligibility clauses (domestic-only, non-red declarations, bid bond), required certifications and their expiry dates, and the likely local partner would replace work that is currently spread over 6–10 sources and two languages.
- Because rules and dates change monthly (FCC Dec 2025 → Jan 2026 → Jul 2026; Taiwan budget Nov 2025 → May → Jun → Aug 2026), every fact in such a brief needs a "verified on" date. That matches Portolan's sourcing rules.
