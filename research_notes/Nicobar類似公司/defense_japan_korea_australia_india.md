# Nicobar-like boutique defense / UAS / aerospace market-entry consultancies: Japan, South Korea, Australia, India

Benchmark: Nicobar Group was a ~10-person Shanghai consultancy that helped North American vendors enter China's state-dominated nuclear market. Its services were market intelligence, lead generation and HAF604 certification help. The Lantau Group acquired its team in 2019.
Criteria: (a) small (≤~50 staff), single-industry market-entry consultancy; (b) helps foreign vendors into a government/SOE-dominated, certification-gated market; (c) market intelligence / lead gen / certification or qualification help / local execution; (d) spans two countries or has a partner on the other side.
Scope notes:
- Firms already covered in reports/海外市場進入產品比較.md and research_notes/海外市場進入產品比較/ are excluded: Usul, Locke, Capto, Clipper Defense, FLT Consulting, GIA, Proxyppon, Foremark, Vantage, IndusBridge/FedTech and others.
- Law firms and big consultancies (Kim & Chang, Yulchon, Lee & Ko, Jipyong, MinterEllison, PwC, A&M, CT Group, Cohen Group) are named only as context.
- Headcount and founding year marked "(data vendor)" come from Exa's LinkedIn-derived company profiles. Treat them as **low reliability**.
- Several sites could not be fetched directly: the WebFetch network allowlist blocked them. They were read through the Exa fetch tool instead.
- Status is as of Nov 2026.

## Q1. Which boutique defense market-entry / offset / industrial-participation advisors serve foreign OEMs in Japan, Korea, Australia and India?

### Takeaway
About 20 small firms (1–50 staff) fit the Nicobar pattern. At least 14 meet 3 or more of criteria (a)–(d). The closest analogues are:
- **Japan:** Nexus Pacific and PacForce.
- **Korea:** Global Konnection and Delta One.
- **Australia:** Arican, USAPAC, 127 East, Forsythia, Axia Dynamis and GDI Strategies.
- **India:** DAC Intl, Crescendo Defence and Sugosha.

Most were founded in 2020–2025, which suggests the category is new and growing in Japan and Australia. India's firms are older and focused on offsets.

### Inferences
- **Japan and Australia** are seeing a wave of new boutiques: Nexus Pacific (2025), Axia Dynamis (2025), PNS (2025), JADC (2025), PacForce (2023), Sentinel (2023), Greyhaven (2023). The timing matches Japan's 2% of GDP push and AUKUS Pillar II. Most have 2–7 staff, which is smaller than Nicobar's roughly 10.
- **Japan's** structural gatekeeper is the sogo shosha. Boutiques therefore sell "which trading house / which business unit" selection rather than direct lead generation. This is a Nicobar-like intelligence plus partnering wedge.
- **Korea's** boutiques are mostly ex-Hanwha/Samsung or US-alliance veterans who work both directions (Global Konnection, Delta One). Law firms (Kim & Chang, Yulchon, Lee & Ko, Jipyong) dominate DAPA and offset work at the high end.
- **India's** firms are offset- and partner-search-centric. Agent rules make a pure "representative on commission" model risky (see Q2), so most present themselves as "advisory".
- The best-documented UAS-specific signals are Nexus Pacific (soliciting UAS/C-UAS clients), Greyhaven (Shield AI), Axia Dynamis (C-UAS), and Future Zen and Crescendo (drone and unmanned clients).

### Gaps
- Founding year, headcount or HQ city is not public for Global Konnection, Tech Diplomat, Forsythia, USAPAC, 127 East, Crescendo and Sugosha.
- No independent press coverage was found for Nexus Pacific or Global Konnection; the evidence is self-description only.
- No Japan-specific firm was found that offers help with ATLA's competitive-participation qualification (競争参加資格) registration as a named service.
- English and limited Korean/Japanese searching only. Korean-language boutiques may be undercounted.

## Q2. Fee models (retainer, success fee, commission; India's agent rules) and public prices

### Takeaway
No firm in the four target markets publishes a price. The public fee models are:
- **retainer** (127 East, USAPAC)
- **fixed-price scopes** (GDIS)
- multi-month engagements (Nexus Pacific's 12–18 months)

Commission and success fees are tightly regulated:
- In **India**, conditional (contingent) agent contracts are barred under DAP 2020.
- In **Korea**, defence trade agents must register with DAPA and report commissions.
This is why boutiques avoid the success-fee model in government defense sales.

### Cited Findings
- **127 East (Australia):** "Retainer-based local presence (market updates, customer engagement, trade show attendance)" — [127 East](https://127east.com.au/service-04.html)
- **USAPAC (Australia):** "A senior retained engagement" with monthly executive briefings — [USAPAC](https://usapac.com.au/)
- **GDIS (Australia):** "fixed-price scopes of work with clearly defined milestones, deliverables and timeframes" — [GDIS](https://gdistrategies.com/)
- **Nexus Pacific (Japan):** manages "12 to 18 months of consensus-building that Japanese procurement requires". This implies long engagements; no price is given — [Nexus Pacific](https://nexuspacific.org/)
- **Comparator (US-based, covers APAC, outside the four markets' HQs):** Bastion Atlas (Fort Mill SC, founded 2024, 4 staff, data vendor) publishes "$999/month" for its SONAR intelligence reports, plus fixed-fee strategy products. It says "No hourly billing, ever" and caps BD at 10 active clients — [Bastion Atlas profile](https://bastionatlas.com/)
- **India, DAP 2020 agents:**
  - The engagement or termination of an agent must be disclosed to MoD within 2 weeks, with all payments made to the agent in the previous year. "Conditional contracts are barred", and MoD can reject or remove an agent at any stage — [Lexology (Nishith Desai), Nov 2022](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=6c22175a-e419-4993-ac09-0d2728614408)
  - The DAP RFP format includes an "Information Proforma: Engagement of Agents by Foreign Vendors" — [DAP 2020 Vol 1, MoD](https://www.ddpmod.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/dap-2020-11-nov-21_0_0.pdf)
  - Recommended practice is to appoint only MoD-registered agents, avoid offshore payments, and restrict consultants to "back-end support, with no direct communication with the MoD" — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=6c22175a-e419-4993-ac09-0d2728614408)
  - The Integrity Pact requires foreign bidders to disclose Indian agents and payments to intermediaries — [CVC circular](https://cgda.gov.in/ifa/orders/cvccir220809.pdf)
  - In a model tender (non-MoD), Indian agents' commission must be disclosed and paid in INR in India on satisfactory completion — [Model Tender Document](https://cdnbbsr.s3waas.gov.in/s316026d60ff9b54410b3435b403afd226/uploads/2023/02/2023021743.pdf)
  - DRDO says "It is not the policy of Government per se to look for, encourage or engage agents". Indian agents must enlist and disclose their commission — [DRDO Procurement Manual 2016](https://www.cmai.asia/pdf/DRDO%20procurement-manual-2016.pdf)
- **India offsets (affects demand for offset advisors):**
  - DAP 2020 offsets apply to Buy (Global) cases with AoN of ₹2,000 crore or more
  - G2G and ab-initio single-vendor cases are excluded
  - The offset contract is separate
  - Administered by DOMW
  - Sources: [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/government-contracts-procurement-ppp/1016564/offset-obligations-under-the-defence-acquisition-procedure-2020); [Defense Mirror](https://defensemirror.com/news/27953/India_Removes_Offset_For_Government_To_Government_Arms_Deals)
- **Korea agents:**
  - A "defence trade agent" (군수품무역대리업) acting for a foreign company in DAPA contracts must register with DAPA in advance (resumes, staffing, integrity and security pledges). Acting unregistered is punishable. For procurements over US$2M a foreign contractor is generally expected to deal directly with DAPA — [Lexology (Kim & Chang), 2019](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=7fa985c1-3264-4976-8f11-9a1e06579a88)
  - Defense Acquisition Program Act Art. 57-4 requires registered agents to report brokerage commissions (중개수수료) above a decree-set project size to DAPA. The current text is effective 2 Oct 2026 — [govbrief.kr](https://govbrief.kr/scan/010107/57%EC%9D%984/)
  - The 2016 amendment introduced agent registration and commission reporting — [Money Today, May 2016](https://www.mt.co.kr/politics/2016/05/19/2016051912347620813)
- **Korea offsets:**
  - Foreign contractors sign an MOU with Korean Industry Participants and an MOA with DAPA's Offset Division — [DAPA](https://www.dapa.go.kr/dapa_en/sub.do?menuId=822)
  - Offsets can apply above US$10M — [trade.gov CCG](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/south-korea-defense-industry-equipment)
  - "Value Banking" lets foreign firms bank offset credit in advance — [KOTRA Express 2026](https://www.investkorea.org/upload/kotraexpress/2026/04/images/Special_Contribution.pdf)

### Inferences
- In India and Korea, a boutique taking a success fee on a government sale is legally treated as an "agent". It must register or disclose, and in India contingent fees are barred. The workable Nicobar-style fee model in these markets is therefore a retainer or fixed-fee advisory, with success fees only on private-sector partnering or offset-partner deals.
- Australia and Japan have no comparable agent registry in the sources found. Retainers ("fractional BD", "local rep") are the norm.

### Gaps
- No public price or day rate was found for any firm in the four markets.
- No sourced Japan rule on agent or commission disclosure for MOD/ATLA was found.
- Australia's DISP fees and consultant prices for DISP help were not found.

## Q3. Acquired or closed examples

### Takeaway
The clearest Nicobar-like exit is **Pacific Aerospace Consulting's Australian arm joining Aurizn in Nov 2024**: a veteran-owned boutique founded in Canberra in 2005 with a US sister company, acquired by a PE-backed Tier 2. Japan shows the corporate-spin-off variant (JADC under SHIFT; PNS from NASCO). No closures were confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- **Pacific Aerospace Consulting (PAC) → Aurizn:**
  - The Australian arm, established in Canberra in 2005 with a US sister organisation, joined Adelaide-based Aurizn (backed by Pemba Capital Partners). This made Aurizn a Tier 2 with "over 300 professionals"
  - Aurizn said it would look at acquiring PAC's American business too
  - PAC co-founder and US CEO Eric Rambow became an Aurizn executive director
  - PAC was noted for "assisting the transfer of capabilities between the US and Australia"
  - Source: [Consultancy.com.au, 19 Nov 2024](https://www.consultancy.com.au/news/10283/pacific-aerospace-consulting-joins-defence-company-aurizn)
- **McR Defence → Aurizn (2024):** Aurizn earlier that year "added commercial specialist McR Defence from law firm McCullough Robertson", bringing in Brett Sangster as CEO — [Consultancy.com.au](https://www.consultancy.com.au/news/10283/pacific-aerospace-consulting-joins-defence-company-aurizn)
- **Arican–DIA partnership (Oct 2025):** a partnership, not an acquisition. It is the Australia–US "partner on the other side" pattern — [ADM](https://www.australiandefence.com.au/news/news/arican-partners-with-us-based-dia)
- **JADC (Japan):** formed 1 Apr 2025 as a 100% SHIFT Inc. subsidiary out of SHIFT's defense business, which launched in 2022 — [JADC](https://shift-jadc.jp/en/company/)
- **PNS (Japan):** spun off from Nihon Aircraft Supply in Feb 2025 — [PNS profile](https://polaris-ns.co.jp/)

### Inferences
- The acquirer pattern differs from Nicobar→Lantau (a sector consultancy buying a sector consultancy). In Australia, PE-backed defence-tech roll-ups buy BD and engineering boutiques. In Japan, IT or trading groups spin defense units into dedicated companies.
- Insighteon (India) and Offset India Solutions have no recent public activity. They may be dormant, but closure is unconfirmed.

### Gaps
- No acquisition of a Korean or Indian defense market-entry boutique was found.
- The PAC/Aurizn deal value was not public.
- Whether Aurizn later acquired PAC's US business was not found.
